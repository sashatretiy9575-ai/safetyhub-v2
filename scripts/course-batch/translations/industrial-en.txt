Industrial Safety|Industrial hazards and the organisation of safe work.|Lifting equipment, pressure equipment and electrical installations.|Accident prevention, worker actions and production control.|A training course for work in the Republic of Kazakhstan.
What the course covers|General requirements apply across production operations.|Each equipment section covers documentation, authorisation and protective measures.|Practical scenarios help identify conditions that require work to stop.|Equipment-specific authorisation is arranged separately.
Purpose of industrial safety|Industrial safety protects people, property and the environment from hazardous production factors.|Accident prevention begins before equipment starts.|Emergency preparedness includes communication, evacuation and rehearsed personnel actions.|Safety must be maintained throughout the equipment life cycle.
Regulatory framework|The Kazakhstan Law on Civil Protection establishes general requirements.|Order No. 359 covers lifting equipment; Order No. 358 covers pressure equipment.|Electrical safety and hot work are governed by their specific rules.|Work instructions account for the equipment and site conditions.|A sample instruction cannot replace requirements for the actual task.
Hazardous production facilities|Hazardous substances and processes require checking the criteria for a hazardous facility.|Identification follows legislation rather than the everyday name of a business.|Hazardous technical devices are assessed according to their type and parameters.|Workers must know their area's hazards and the limits of their authorisation.|A facility name without the word hazardous does not remove the risk.
Hazardous substances and processes|Combustible substances can ignite; oxidisers intensify combustion.|Toxic substances are hazardous through inhalation and contact.|Pressure, temperature and mechanical energy can cause severe injury.|Work requires information about the substance, process and protective measures.|Hazards depend on properties and handling conditions.
Accidents and incidents|Destruction, an uncontrolled explosion or a hazardous release can indicate an accident.|Equipment failure or a process deviation can constitute an incident.|An event must be reported and examined even if nobody is injured.|Concealing a deviation prevents the organisation from averting a later accident.|Record deviations before they recur.
Duties of the organisation|The organisation maintains equipment and meets mandatory requirements.|It appoints responsible persons and establishes control procedures.|It arranges training, knowledge assessment and personnel authorisation.|It maintains readiness to contain accidents and deal with their consequences.|Appointing a responsible person does not remove the organisation's duties.
Worker actions|Workers perform assigned tasks within their training and authorisation.|They check the workplace and use the specified protective equipment.|They report defects, deviations and threats to people.|In immediate danger, they stop work and move to safety.|Do not attempt an unauthorised repair of a dangerous fault.
Training and authorisation|Training explains hazards and safe work methods.|Knowledge assessment verifies understanding of requirements.|A briefing addresses the actual task and workplace.|Practical preparation and authorisation are required where stipulated by the rules and task.|Viewing a presentation does not authorise every operation.
Production control|Control identifies breaches before an accident occurs.|Checks cover equipment, documentation, personnel and working conditions.|Each finding receives an action, an owner and a deadline.|Closure requires verification that the issue has actually been corrected.|A closed finding needs confirmation on site.
Checks before starting work|Clarify the task, work-zone boundaries and responsible persons.|Check equipment, protective devices and clear access routes.|Assess neighbouring activities and possible changes in conditions.|Do not begin until unsuitable conditions have been corrected.|Yesterday's good condition does not replace today's check.
Workplace risk assessment|Identify what could cause harm and who could enter the danger zone.|Prefer hazard elimination and engineering protection.|Organisational measures and PPE provide additional protection.|Repeat the assessment when equipment, tasks or surroundings change.|Changed conditions require a new assessment.
Work permits and coordination|Use a work permit where required by the rules and established procedures.|Specify the location, task, personnel and protective measures.|Actual workplace conditions must match the conditions of authorisation.|A changed task requires review of the permit conditions, not an informal agreement.|A signature on a permit does not replace completed protective measures.
Isolation of energy sources|Before maintenance, identify electrical, mechanical and stored energy.|Stop equipment and prevent unintended starting.|Release pressure and secure moving parts under the established procedure.|Authorised personnel verify the absence of hazardous energy.|The Stop button alone does not isolate energy.
Hot work|Welding, cutting and heating produce sparks and hot surfaces.|Remove combustible materials or protect them using the specified method.|Check adjacent rooms, openings and concealed spaces.|Provide firefighting equipment and post-work monitoring.|Sparks can travel beyond the visible work area.
Equipment documentation|The equipment passport identifies the device and its permitted parameters.|The manufacturer's manual defines operating and maintenance conditions.|Logs preserve inspection, repair and defect records.|Establish unknown parameters before operating equipment.|A nearby device's parameters cannot replace the equipment passport.
Lifting equipment|Cranes, hoists and other lifting devices have different applications.|Slings, lifting beams and containers form part of the lifting system.|Equipment selection depends on load mass, the work zone and installation conditions.|Plan the operation using the equipment passport and lifting procedure.|Safe lifting depends on the entire system.
Lifting equipment passport and markings|Check the equipment type and identification number.|Verify lifting capacity, installation conditions and manufacturer restrictions.|Check inspection records and authorisation for use.|Clarify illegible or contradictory markings.|Verify lifting capacity for the actual operating conditions.
Recording and maintaining lifting equipment|State registration or internal recording requirements depend on the device type.|Exemption from state registration does not remove control obligations.|Maintain and repair equipment according to the established schedule.|Document inspections and the correction of defects.|Internal records also require clear responsibility.
Responsibilities during crane operations|Supervision oversees safe operation.|The person responsible for serviceable condition organises maintenance and repair.|The person responsible for load handling organises the safe lifting operation.|The crane operator and slinger fulfil their duties and use agreed signals.|Responsibilities must be understood before lifting begins.
Preparing a lift|Determine the load mass, centre of gravity and attachment points.|Select equipment and accessories with suitable characteristics.|Check the route, landing area and absence of people.|Agree on communication between participants.|Establish an unknown load mass before lifting.
Slings and lifting accessories|Check identification and suitability for the chosen lifting arrangement.|Inspect ropes, chains, sling legs, hooks and connections.|Remove damaged or unidentifiable accessories from service.|Protect load edges using the specified padding.|A knot in a sling is not a repair.
Lifting danger zone|Keep people out of the area where a load could fall or move.|Do not allow anyone beneath a suspended load.|Do not guide a load by hand while positioned between it and an obstacle.|Maintain the zone boundaries throughout the operation.|A short lift presents the same hazard.
Signals and stopping|Agree on clear signals and the communication method in advance.|Stop movement if communication is lost.|A stop signal concerning danger to people requires an immediate response.|Resume only after the cause is resolved and actions are coordinated.|Do not guess the meaning of an unclear signal.
Inspection of lifting equipment|Shift inspections, maintenance and technical examinations serve different purposes.|Check the structure, mechanisms, brakes and protective devices.|Determine intervals and scope from the applicable rules and equipment documentation.|Do not bypass a defective limiter to finish a shift.|Protective devices are part of the conditions for safe operation.
Testing and repairing lifting equipment|Qualified specialists conduct tests under the established programme.|A test load does not authorise overloads during normal operation.|Certain repairs or modifications require additional examination.|Record the results and authorisation for continued operation.|A trial lift cannot replace the required post-repair examination.
Welded joints in lifting equipment|Repair safety-critical components using an established procedure.|Determine welder, material and inspection requirements for the structure.|Cracks, deformation and unacceptable defects require specialist assessment.|Unauthorised welding can alter structural behaviour.|Repair of a load-bearing structure requires a specific decision.
Scenario: a damaged sling|A sling is defective, but the load must be moved urgently.|Do not begin the lift; notify the responsible person.|Prevent further issue of the accessory pending the required decision.|Continue with suitable, serviceable equipment.|Urgency does not change acceptance criteria.
Pressure equipment|Boilers, vessels, pipelines and cylinders contain stored energy.|Loss of containment can cause a release, burns or harmful exposure.|Risk depends on pressure, temperature and the working medium.|Operate each device within its established limits.|A small vessel is not necessarily a low-hazard vessel.
Operating and design parameters|Operating, design, permitted and test pressures serve different purposes.|Test pressure applies to an established test procedure.|Use the documented and authorised operating limits.|Observe temperature restrictions and medium compatibility.|A test parameter is not an operating parameter.
Vessel classification and applicable rules|Classification depends on parameters and the nature of the medium.|Pressure alone is insufficient for a universal assessment.|A specialist determines applicable requirements and inspection scope.|Operators use the established restrictions for their own equipment.|Classification affects inspection and documentation.
Protective devices|Safety devices limit dangerous parameter exceedances.|Their condition and settings require established checks.|Do not bypass, blank off or alter protection without authorisation.|Investigate the cause of a protective trip.|Protection is not intended to compensate continually for an incorrect operating mode.
Monitoring pressure and temperature|Compare instrument readings with the permitted operating conditions.|Check for visible damage and unreliable readings.|Replace a faulty instrument under the established procedure.|Respond to leakage, vibration or unusual noise as instructed.|One normal reading is insufficient when other danger signs are present.
Starting and monitoring operation|Confirm equipment readiness and authorisation before starting.|Check valves, protection and the required instruments.|Change parameters in the sequence specified by the instructions.|Respond to deviations as instructed; do not guess settings.|Starting after repair requires confirmed readiness.
Stopping and preparing for maintenance|Isolate equipment from pressure sources and hazardous media.|Release pressure and verify a safe condition.|Clean, purge and test the atmosphere where required.|Begin work after completing protective measures and authorisation.|A faulty gauge reading zero does not prove safety.
Technical examination of vessels|Inspection and testing assess technical condition.|Intervals depend on the device and applicable requirements.|Do not start work when an examination is overdue; notify the responsible person to arrange the examination and authorisation.|Record the results in the equipment documentation.|Do not copy an inspection interval from a different vessel.
Compressed gas cylinders|Identify the gas using markings and documentation.|Protect cylinders against falling, heat and valve damage.|Use compatible regulators and serviceable connections.|Never check leaks with an open flame.|Do not connect a cylinder with uncertain identification.
Repairing and welding vessels|Repair only after safe removal from service.|The procedure, personnel and inspection must suit the vessel's purpose.|Do not weld a container with unidentified residual contents.|Return to service requires documented inspection results.|An apparently empty container may retain explosive residues.
Scenario: rising pressure|Pressure rises and normal control fails to restore the operating conditions.|The operator follows the emergency shutdown instructions.|Move people out of danger and notify the responsible person.|Do not bypass protection or repair equipment under pressure.|Investigate the cause after making the situation safe.
Electrical hazards|Current, electrical arcs and hot components can cause severe harm.|Moisture, damaged insulation and conductive surfaces increase danger.|The absence of sparks or noise does not establish absence of voltage.|Restrict access to electrical installations under the established procedure.|Voltage can remain dangerous without any advance sensation.
Personnel categories and authority|Duties and electrical safety qualification groups determine permitted actions.|A group does not authorise every task without an assignment and authorisation.|Only authorised personnel connect, repair and switch equipment.|Learners must remain within the limits of their preparation.|Knowing a term does not authorise an operation.
Organising work in electrical installations|Determine the required form of authorisation for the specific task.|Appoint responsible persons and the work team.|The person admitting the team checks workplace preparation and gives a briefing.|Record breaks, relocation and completion under the established procedure.|The actual work must match the assigned task.
Work with voltage removed|Authorised personnel carry out the necessary disconnections.|They prevent unintended energisation and display prohibition notices.|They verify absence of voltage and install the required earthing.|They install the necessary barriers and other notices.|Disconnecting and verifying absence of voltage are different actions.
Earthing and protective conductors|The protective conductor is part of the shock-protection system.|Repair damaged connections before work.|Do not replace a protective conductor with an improvised metal connection.|Specialists carry out checks and repairs.|An intact-looking enclosure does not prove effective protection.
Portable equipment|Inspect the enclosure, cable, plug and connections before use.|Select equipment for the environment and follow its instructions.|Stop using equipment with a damaged cable or signs of overheating.|An improvised connection does not replace a serviceable assembly.|Check condition before switching on.
Electrical protective equipment|Select protection for the voltage and nature of the work.|Before use, check integrity and required test markings.|Do not use damaged equipment or equipment with overdue tests.|PPE does not permit bypassing organisational measures.|An unknown test date requires verification.
Work near live parts|Determine boundaries and safe distances for the actual installation.|Maintain them for tools, loads and equipment parts as well as people.|Do not move barriers without authorisation.|If conditions change, stop work until protective measures are reviewed.|Dangerous approach does not require direct contact.
Returning electrical equipment to service|Verify completion and removal of people from the danger zone.|Remove temporary protective measures in the established sequence.|Record completion and authorisation to energise.|Authorised personnel carry out energisation.|A trial start requires the same safety controls.
Scenario: a damaged cable|A portable tool has an exposed conductor.|Stop using it and prevent further energisation.|Notify the responsible person to arrange repair or replacement.|Do not touch the damage or repair the cable while energised.|Applying tape alone does not authorise continued use.
Emergency notification|Report the location, hazard and known information about casualties.|Use the site's established communication channels; call 112 or 101 where needed.|Do not delay reporting while independently searching for the cause.|Warn nearby people without exposing yourself to danger.|The report must enable responders to locate the danger quickly.
Evacuation and assembly point|Use a safe escape route appropriate to the hazard.|Do not return for belongings or enter a closed-off zone.|At the assembly point, report your presence and known missing persons.|Return only when authorised after conditions have been assessed.|Report immediately if a person's whereabouts are unknown.
Safety when giving assistance|First make sure the rescuer is not exposed to danger.|Call emergency services and describe the situation.|Act within your training and follow the dispatcher's instructions.|Do not enter a hazardous atmosphere or touch an energised casualty without appropriate protection.|A second casualty makes rescue more difficult.
Reviewing an event and restarting|Preserve information about signs, timing and circumstances.|Establish causes from facts, including organisational conditions.|Verify corrective measures before resuming work.|Inform personnel about changes to instructions and equipment.|Restart requires correction of the cause, not just the consequences.
Final knowledge assessment|Work requires a clear task, serviceable equipment and valid authorisation.|Lifting defects, pressure leaks and electrical damage require timely stopping.|Emergency actions begin with protecting people and raising the alarm.|Select Start test.|If conditions are uncertain, wait for verification before working.
